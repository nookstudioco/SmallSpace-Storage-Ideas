What actually fits under a bathroom sink

The cabinet under a bathroom sink is an odd shape by design — a drainpipe running down the middle splits the space in two, and most storage products are built for a flat rectangular cabinet that doesn't exist here. The fix is picking pieces built to work around a pipe, not products that assume there isn't one.

1. Pipe-friendly sliding organizer

Approx. $20–35

Built with a gap or slots specifically so it can sit around a drainpipe instead of being blocked by it. This is worth buying before anything else for this space — measure the pipe's position first so you get one with the opening on the correct side.

2. Stackable stair-step shelf

Approx. $15–25

Sits on one side of the pipe and adds a second visible tier, so bottles in the back row aren't hidden behind bottles in the front. Works well for lotions and cleaning sprays you use often enough to want visible, not buried at the bottom of the cabinet.

3. Tension rod for hanging spray bottles

Approx. $8–15

A cheap tension rod wedged inside the cabinet lets you hang spray bottles by their trigger handles, which frees up the entire floor of the cabinet for bins. It's the same low-cost trick apartment dwellers use in kitchen cabinets, just scaled down for a smaller space.

4. Clear pull-out bins with handles

Approx. $18–30

Anything stored flat on the cabinet floor behind the pipe becomes invisible within a week. Bins with handles let you pull the whole thing forward instead of reaching blindly into the back corner, and clear sides mean you can see what's inside before you commit to digging.

5. Adhesive hooks for the inside of the cabinet door

Approx. $10–15

The inside of the cabinet door itself is almost always empty. A couple of adhesive hooks hold a hair dryer or cleaning gloves off the cabinet floor entirely, which is often the single item taking up the most usable space in there.

Work around the pipes, not against them

Measure the pipe's position and the cabinet's depth before buying anything — most under-sink organizers fail because they were bought to fit an average cabinet, not the one actually behind your doors.
