The bathroom shelf you don't need a drill for

Rental bathrooms tend to give you one medicine cabinet and zero counter space, and the walls are almost always tiled — which means drilling isn't just against the lease, it can crack the tile and cost you the deposit outright. Every pick here stands on its own, wedges into place, or sticks with adhesive strips instead.

1. Over-the-toilet storage rack

Approx. $30–55

Straddles the toilet tank on its own legs, so it needs zero wall contact. This is usually the single biggest storage gain in a small bathroom, since it turns dead air above the toilet into two or three shelves for towels, toiletries, and toilet paper. Check the width against your toilet tank before buying — most are adjustable, but narrow tanks need the compact version.

2. Tension pole shower caddy

Approx. $25–40

Wedges floor-to-ceiling using spring tension, so there's no suction cup that loses grip and no wall bracket to install. It holds far more than a hanging caddy and works in showers where the tile is too textured for suction cups to stick to at all.

3. Adhesive wall-mounted shower organizer

Approx. $10–20

Command-strip style adhesive holds surprisingly well on tile as long as the surface is clean and dry before you stick it, and it comes off without residue at move-out. Good for shampoo bottles and razors that don't need to sit inside the tub itself, freeing up the tub ledge.

4. Slim rolling bathroom storage cart

Approx. $30–50

A narrow 3-tier cart slides into the gap beside the toilet or sink that's too tight for regular furniture. Being on wheels means you can pull it out fully to clean behind it, which fixed shelving never allows, and you can roll it out of the way entirely when you have guests.

5. Medicine cabinet organizer trays

Approx. $12–25

Most medicine cabinets have one shelf and a lot of wasted height above whatever's sitting on it. Stackable trays split that height into two usable levels, the same trick as a cabinet shelf riser in the kitchen, so small items like skincare bottles and floss stop tipping over every time the door opens.

Work around the tile, not through it

Every pick above avoids the two things that get renters in trouble in a bathroom: drilling into tile and leaving adhesive residue behind. Test any adhesive strip on a small hidden patch of tile first if you're not sure how the finish will react.
